## Abstract

On June 2, 2023, Chinese President Xi Jinping delivered a keynote speech at a symposium on cultural inheritance and development in Beijing. In the speech, he pointed out that the outstanding inclusiveness of Chinese civilization determines the open-mindedness of Chinese culture to accept and embrace the world civilization. The cultural exchange between China and the world has never stopped. Chinese culture and world cultures feed off each other. In recent years, the attention paid to the overseas dissemination and translation of Chinese culture has grown much more extensive, and the same is true for the exchange of material and ideas between China and the rest of the world. At a profound level, the agents of civilization interact through the pathways of interlingual communication, ultimately realizing crosscountry, cross-linguistic and cross-cultural integration. This new phenomenon of cultural interaction transcends geography, language, and race, as well as politics and cognition. In this context, we have entitled this special issue “Matter, Knowledge and Culture: Cultural Inheritance and Interlingual Communication.” This special issue presents a variety of in-depth perspectives on exchange and mutual learning between interlingual agents of civilization. The studies bring to light the way the dissemination and exchange of personnel, material, and knowledge that has lasted from the Song and Yuan dynasties through to contemporary times has reinforced communication and mutual learning between civilizations. Recent studies focus on substances that played a role in the history of cultural exchange between China and the outside world, such as books, gold, seashells, harnesses, and porcelain, whence we can observe the multifaceted interaction of civilizations. Two articles in this special issue focus on such vehicles of civilization as tea, books of music, spices, festive foods, and tea books, with a view to depicting Chinese civilization’s three-dimensional communication and interaction with its neighbors and indeed, the world. In “The East Asian Cultural Sphere of Time in Japanese Music Books,” studying the relationship of music and dance with the times and festivals revealed in Japanese music books can help restore the context of the musical rituals and customs lost in their Chinese homeland.
