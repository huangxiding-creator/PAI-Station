12Abstract: Currently, large-scale international EPC engineering projects often 
13suffer from significant cost overruns and major schedule delays due to a lack of 
14comprehensive and systematic identification and quantitative analysis of factors 
15affecting Objective Control from a holistic perspective. This often leads to 
16conflicting management objectives and difficulties in effectively allocating limited 
17resources, resulting in many projects being out of control. This study aims to 
18systematically identify, construct, and quantify key factors that affect Objective 
19Control in large-scale international EPC projects, providing a strategic framework 
20to improve the effectiveness of multi-objective control and contribute to the 
21overall success and sustainable development of international contractors. Initially, 
22this study employs grounded theory to systematically encode secondary data such 
23as relevant literature and case reports, initially constructing a systematic 
24theoretical model of "EPC comprehensive management capability". Subsequently, 
25exploratory factor analysis (EFA) is used to analyze data collected from EPC 
26professionals, verifying and improving the structure, reliability, and validity of the 
27model, ensuring robust measurement, and determining the relative weight of 
28identified factors.This study establishes and validates a hierarchical model of 'EPC 
29Integrated Management Capability', identifying design leadership (27.13%) and 
30international supply chain management (25.68%) as the two most critical pillars, 
31significantly surpassing construction execution capability (21.09%). Notably, the 
32rationality of procurement planning emerges as the most influential single factor. 
33These findings empirically validate the 'front-end loading' principle and indicate a 
34paradigm shift from construction-centric control to a strategic focus on integrated 
35design and supply chain management. The primary theoretical contribution of this 
36study is the development of a new, empirically validated measurement scale for 
37EPC Integrated Management Capability, advancing EPC governance theory by 
38quantifying the core integrated structure. In practice, this study provides 
39actionable insights for international EPC turnkey contractors: it emphasizes the 
40necessity of elevating procurement planning to a strategic level, investing in 
41innovative design teams, formalizing design-construction supply chain integration,

42and cultivating resilient global supplier networks to effectively navigate the 
43complexities of the international market.

461. Introduction  
47In the context of accelerating globalization and economic integration, the 
48demand for large and complex engineering projects in the international 
49engineering market is rapidly increasing. As a crucial component of the project 
50owner's overall investment plan, failure to deliver the project according to the 
51predetermined objective can have a significant impact on the entire investment 
52plan. Therefore, the owner aims to determine the project investment at the project 
53initiation stage, put the project into use according to the predetermined plan, and 
54bear less risk. The EPC model, as an integrated project management model that 
55integrates design, procurement, and construction, can effectively integrate 
56project resources, shorten the construction period, optimize cost control, improve 
57project quality, and transfer the responsibility of the entire process management 
58of the project to the general contractor. The contractor bears most of the risks 
59while also gaining higher profit opportunities, making the EPC model one of the 
60mainstream models in the international engineering construction field [1].
61However, many large international EPC projects face severe challenges in 
62achieving their core objectives, often resulting in schedule delays, cost overruns, 
63and quality defects. A report by McKinsey & Company emphasizes that large 
64international engineering projects typically exceed their budgets by 20-45% and 
65lag behind schedule by 40-60% [2]. These failures in objective control directly lead 
66to financial losses for contracting companies, erode profitability, and even 
67threaten their survival and sustainable development in the international market  [3]. 
68Recent research has shown that the instability of global supply chains has further 
69exacerbated these challenges in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic and 
70geopolitical conflicts, significantly increasing uncertainty in cost and schedule [4]. 
71The scientific issues leading to the failure of large international EPC projects are 
72threefold. Firstly, the objectives of large international EPC projects are 
73multifaceted and often conflicting, including cost, time, quality, safety, and 
74environmental sustainability, posing significant challenges to integrated 
75management [5]. Secondly, existing research often addresses these influencing 
76factors in a fragmented manner, lacking a comprehensive and systematic 
77identification from a systems perspective. This results in objective control factor 
78indicators that are neither comprehensive nor systematically classified [6]. Thirdly, 
79there is a notable lack of research on quantitatively analyzing objective control 
80influencing factors to determine their weights. Without clarity on which factors 
81are most critical, it is difficult for project managers to effectively allocate limited 
82resources for preemptive and objectiveed control measures [7].
83Therefore, this study aims to bridge this gap by systematically investigating 
84the factors that affect the objective control of large international EPC projects. 
85The overall objective is to enhance the effectiveness of multi-objective control, 
86thereby contributing to the overall success of these projects and promoting the 
87healthy development of international contracting enterprises. To achieve the 
88established objectives, the specific objectives of this study are: to identify and

89establish a system of factors affecting objective control in large international EPC 
90projects, verify the rationality and effectiveness of the identified factors, and

942.Literature review
952.1 Large-Scale International EPC Projects
96Large international EPC projects represent a complex and high-risk form of 
97project delivery in the global engineering and construction sector. Rudolf et al. 
98(2018) combined large projects with the EPC model in their risk research and 
99summarized nine characteristics of large EPC projects: costs ranging from 
100hundreds of millions to billions of dollars, adoption of the EPC model, complex 
101technology, numerous interfaces, long construction periods, significant impact, 
102numerous stakeholders, project uniqueness and uncertainty of changes, and high 
103execution risk [8]. Compared to traditional project models, the EPC model 
104integrates design, procurement, and construction under one contractor, aiming 
105to reduce interface disputes and responsibility gaps. However, the 
106"international" dimension brings complexities beyond the technical scope of the 
107project. Existing research extensively documents the unique challenges of these 
108projects, including political and regulatory risks, exchange rate fluctuations, 
109cross-cultural management obstacles, and vulnerabilities in cross-border supply 
110chains [9]. Recent research further emphasizes the increasing impact of 
111geopolitical tensions and global supply chain disruptions on project feasibility [10]. 
112Hwang (2023) emphasizes that the post-pandemic era has exacerbated these 
113risks, severely impacting project objective control [11]. Therefore, the success of 
114large international EPC engineering projects is unstable and highly susceptible 
115to numerous external and internal variables.
1162.2 Project Objective Control
117In the context of international EPC projects, the concept of project objective 
118control has evolved beyond the traditional "iron triangle" of cost, time, and 
119quality to encompass broader objectives such as health and safety, 
120environmental sustainability, and stakeholder satisfaction. Numerous studies 
121have attempted to identify the factors influencing these objectives. For instance, 
122a study by Siddiqui, M.A.W et al. (2022) on large infrastructure projects in India 
123identified design changes and poor resource planning as key factors contributing 
124to schedule delays [12]. Tshidavhu et al. (2020) conducted an in-depth analysis of 
125major energy projects in South Africa against the backdrop of widespread cost 
126and schedule overruns in global large-scale projects, finding that delayed

127decision-making by owners and a shortage of skilled labor were key reasons for 
128poor cost and schedule control [13]. Denicol et al. (2020) categorized the main 
129reasons for poor project performance into six themes through a literature review: 
130decision-making behavior, project governance, risk, team capability, stakeholder 
131management, and supply chain integration and coordination  [14] . Recent 
132empirical research has begun to focus on sustainability objectives. For example, 
133Fernando Y (2022) noted that under strict green regulations, integrating circular 
134economy principles into the supply chain has become a new key factor affecting 
135project success [15].Although these studies offer valuable insights, they often 
136adopt an isolated approach, investigating factors that affect a single objective 
137such as cost and schedule, without fully addressing the interconnectedness of 
138these objectives in an integrated EPC environment. This fragmented approach 
139fails to provide an overall view of how control over one objective affects and is 
140affected by other objectives.
1412.3 EPC comprehensive management capability
142 The concept of integrated management is often regarded as the core 
143 advantage of the EPC model. Literature indicates that successful project 
144 outcomes are not solely determined by the ability to control individual tasks, 
145 but also hinge on the contractor's overall capacity to manage the project as a 
146 unified system. Scholars have touched upon the elements of this capacity, 
147 often referring to aspects such as "organizational capability", "project 
148 management maturity", "interface management", or "integrated 
149 management" [16,17]. However, in existing research, the concept of "EPC 
150 comprehensive management capability" remains largely uncertain and 
151 qualitative. It is often described as a principle rather than formalized into a 
152 quantifiable framework with clearly defined dimensions and measurable 
153 indicators. This lack of formalization makes it difficult to systematically 
154 evaluate, link to project outcomes, and ultimately enhance this capability in 
155 practice. Although scholars such as Mensah S (2018) have attempted to 
156 construct a capability framework for project resilience through case studies, 
157 its direct quantitative relationship with multi-objective control has not been 
158 fully explored [18].
1592.4 Existing research methods
160 Currently, various methods are employed for EPC project control. 
161 Qualitative methods, such as case studies and expert interviews, are prevalent 
162 in deeply exploring complex phenomena and initially identifying factors. 
163 Quantitative methods, particularly questionnaire surveys and statistical 
164 analyses (such as factor analysis and regression), are widely used to verify 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPTARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
165 hypotheses and examine relationships between variables. Additionally, studies 
166 have utilized the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and Decision Making Trial 
167 and Evaluation Lab (DEMATEL) to analyze risk factors. For instance, Rezaee 
168 M J (2019) employed the fuzzy DEMATEL model to analyze the causal 
169 relationships among factors contributing to schedule delays in international 
170 projects [19]. While these methods represent an advancement, their application 
171 is often limited to analyzing a predefined list of non-systematic factors. Few 
172 studies first establish a comprehensive factor system based on a formalized 
173 capability framework and then utilize these robust quantitative methods to 
174 determine the global weight of all influencing factors within the scope of 
175 overall project control.
176 A comprehensive review of existing literature reveals that previous 
177 studies have identified influencing factors in a fragmented manner, often 
178 focusing on isolated objectives or specific project phases, lacking a 
179 comprehensive and systematic identification of the entire process and 
180 integrated objective control factors of international EPC projects. The key 
181 concepts of EPC comprehensive management capabilities have not yet been 
182 transformed into quantifiable and measurable theoretical structures, nor have 
183 the weights of influencing factors of integrated comprehensive management 
184 capabilities been fully quantified, making it impossible to prioritize 
185 management interventions strategically. The originality of this study lies in 
186 formally conceptualizing and implementing comprehensive EPC management 
187 capabilities as a quantifiable framework with direct theoretical and 
188 management relevance, deriving a comprehensive system of objective control 
189 influencing factors, and verifying its factor structure and calculating the 
190 global weight of each factor. This provides project managers with a diagnostic 
191 and strategic tool that enables them to focus resources on strengthening the 
192 core capabilities that are most important for the success of large international 
193 EPC projects.
1943.Methodology
195 This study systematically combines the qualitative method of grounded 
196 theory with the quantitative method of factor analysis to construct a coherent 
197 "exploration-validation" mixed research framework. Firstly, grounded theory 
198 is employed to systematically encode a vast amount of secondary data, such as 
199 literature and case reports. From the rich practical experience texts, 
200 comprehensive, detailed, and realistic influencing factors emerge, thus 
201 initially constructing a systematic theoretical model of "EPC comprehensive 
202 management capability". Then, exploratory factor analysis (EFA) is introduced

ARTICLE IN PRESS
203 to verify the structural validity of numerous factors in the initial model, and 
204 possibly eliminate those statistically unstable indicators, making the final 
205 indicator system more concise, efficient, and reliable. The combination of 
206 methods in this study overcomes the "blind spots" that may exist in 
207 quantitative research with the "openness" of grounded theory, and overcomes 
208 the "subjective bias" that may exist in qualitative research with the "objective 
209 statistics" of factor analysis. It is suitable for studying the systematic issues of 
210 complex management, such as objective control in large international EPC 
211 projects. The overall idea of this study is shown in Figure 1.

2143.1 survey development
215Given the objective complexity and subjective capability limitations of 
216large-scale international EPC projects, this paper opts for a case analysis 
217approach to identify them. To ensure the representativeness of the cases in 
218international EPC projects, this study sets a criterion that all selected projects 
219have a contract amount exceeding US$450 million, ensuring that the research 
220subjects are "large-scale" projects that align with the theme of this study. 
221Additionally, all selected projects are internationally recognized to be under the 
222EPC or EPC variant contract model, ensuring consistency in the research context. 
223To ensure the heterogeneity and wide applicability of the cases and avoid 
224one-sided conclusions, the selected cases cover multiple regions with different 
225political, economic, cultural, and regulatory environments, spanning various 
226industries such as transportation infrastructure, energy and chemical engineering, 
227and industrial facilities. Furthermore, all cases share common international 
228project characteristics and face typical challenges in international engineering,

3844.Results 
3854.1 Analysis of Weight Influence
386The data results demonstrate the dominance of design and supply chain 
387management factors, validating the core philosophy of EPC: front-end loading is 
388crucial, and the impact of design and procurement on overall project success is 
389greater than that of on-site execution alone. During these early stages, the cost 
390of errors, delays, or suboptimal decisions grows exponentially, making it difficult 
391to correct during construction, especially in an international environment with 
392complex logistics and diverse standards.
393Design leadership capability is the primary factor influencing the objective 
394control of large international EPC projects, accounting for 27.13% of the weight. 
395This underscores the notion that "design is the driving force in EPC projects," 
396serving as the strategic shaper of the overall project value and the primary 
397gateway for risk control. Large international EPC projects often encounter 
398extreme climates, special geological conditions, stringent environmental 
399standards, or specific technical specifications set by the owner, making 
400standardized solutions ineffective. Design originality capability directly 
401determines the technical feasibility, safety, and long-term stability of the project, 
402thus earning the highest weight of 9.75%. Design optimization can significantly 
403reduce construction difficulty, shorten the construction period, and mitigate 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPTARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
404safety risks, serving as a crucial guarantee for achieving project progress 
405objectives. Under the EPC lump sum contract, cost savings brought about by 
406optimization are directly converted into the contractor's profits. Therefore, 
407design optimization capability is the core link that transforms the "possibility" of 
408original solutions into the "profitability" of the project, accounting for 9.26% of 
409the weight. Design integration management capability ensures that optimized 
410solutions can be seamlessly integrated and efficiently executed in the 
411procurement and construction processes, with design serving as the "leader" 
412rather than an "isolated island." Hence, design integration management 
413capability is the strategic core that drives the integrated management of large 
414international EPC projects, accounting for 9.07% of the weight.
415International supply chain management capability stands as the second pillar 
416of project objective control, carrying a weight of 25.68%. This finding 
417underscores the unprecedented complexity and fragility of the supply chain in 
418international EPC projects amidst globalization. The reliability and efficiency of 
419the "material flow" have become as crucial as the design capability of the 
420"technology flow", together forming the two strategic engines for project success. 
421Among these, the rationality of procurement planning emerges as the most 
422influential single factor, carrying a weight of 10.09%. A reasonable procurement 
423plan outlines the delivery cycle of key equipment, cost commitments, and 
424ensures a seamless transition from design to construction, serving as the 
425primary line of defense against long cycles and uncertainties, as well as 
426managing financial and compliance risks. Enhancing the rationality of 
427procurement planning is the cornerstone and primary lever point for effectively 
428controlling the three major objectives of cost, schedule, and quality in large 
429international EPC projects. Secondly, suppliers play a fundamental role in 
430controlling equipment quality from the source and avoiding performance 
431disputes. General contractors can stimulate suppliers' initiative in technology 
432optimization, cost savings, and emergency response through early intervention, 
433long-term agreements, and other methods. Therefore, supplier selection and 
434management account for 9.51% of the weight, reflecting the key role of partner 
435reliability in global procurement. Logistics warehousing and process supervision 
436account for relatively low weights of 8.94% and 8.57%, respectively, possibly 
437because they are more at the execution level.
438Among the three core capabilities of international EPC projects, construction 
439performance capability holds the lowest weight, accounting for 21.09%. This 
440reflects the shift in focus towards risk management and value creation in large 
441international EPC projects. Under the EPC model, design leadership capability 
442maximizes constructability, avoids major technical risks, and achieves cost 
443control through optimization. International supply chain management ensures 
444that key equipment and materials arrive at the site on time, with guaranteed 
445quality and quantity. Notably, the technical capability of construction (9.09%) 
446holds a higher weight than management capability (8.64%), indicating that in 
447international projects, the ability to solve complex technical problems is 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPTARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
448paramount. Resource integration capability has the lowest weight (8.00%), as it 
449is a fundamental capability that all qualified contractors should possess in 
450international EPC projects, rather than a competitive advantage. Consequently, 
451the role of the construction team has evolved from being the "leader" of the 
452project to the "final implementer" of the integrated process. Its core value lies in 
453using its technical capabilities to address residual risks and uncertainties that 
454have not been completely eliminated in the front-end links. Contractors should 
455not focus their main management efforts on post-construction supervision, but 
456should proactively invest in improving design optimization and supply chain 
457planning capabilities. Simultaneously, when forming a construction team, 
458priority should be given to selecting and relying on key personnel with strong 
459technical capabilities and rich international experience.
4604.2 Inter-group comparison analysis of robustness check
461 To deepen the analysis and test the robustness of these findings, this study 
462 divided the respondents into two groups based on their job roles: management 
463 personnel and technical personnel (including designers), and conducted a 
464 comparative analysis of their respective weights. Both groups ranked design 
465 leadership and supply chain management as the top two important indicators, 
466 revealing a strong consensus on the overall hierarchical structure and 
467 confirming the robustness of the overall model. In addition, management 
468 personnel placed more emphasis on international supply chain management 
469 (average 27.1%), especially the rationality of procurement planning (10.8%). 
470 This indicates that management teams view projects from the perspective of 
471 system integration and risk mitigation, where supply chain is the greatest 
472 source of uncertainty in large and complex international engineering projects, 
473 prompting them to prioritize factors that ensure predictable flow of materials 
474 and equipment to the site. In contrast, technical personnel emphasized design 
475 leadership (average 28.5%), especially design creativity and optimization. For 
476 them, technological excellence, innovation, and the inherent quality and 
477 efficiency of design are the main levers to prevent rework, overcome 
478 execution obstacles, and achieve project objectives. These two perspectives 
479 still converge on the high importance of these areas, strengthening the 
480 conclusion that EPC integrated management is fundamentally closely coupled 
481 with design and supply chain.
4825.Discussion
483 This study translates the abstract concept of 'integration' in EPC projects 
484 into a measurable and weighted structure by proposing and empirically 
485 validating a hierarchical model of 'EPC comprehensive management 
486 capability'. Firstly, this study provides a quantifiable framework for evaluating 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPTARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
487 integration capability by decomposing it into three core capabilities and 11 
488 measurable third-level indicators. Secondly, the relative weight distribution 
489 challenges the over-reliance on the construction-centered control paradigm. 
490 This provides empirical support for the 'front-end loading' theory and extends 
491 it to the international EPC context, indicating that comprehensive 
492 management must be strategically biased towards design and procurement to 
493 be effective. The weight of construction performance is lower than that of 
494 upstream peers, indicating that in the complex international EPC environment, 
495 project control is more about active coordination of knowledge-intensive 
496 (design) and globally dispersed (supply chain) processes than passive 
497 supervision of on-site activities. The key resources and capabilities for 
498 contractors to gain competitive advantage are not primarily their construction 
499 personnel, but their intellectual capital for innovative design and global 
500 network orchestration capabilities.
501 This study offers actionable strategies for international EPC general 
502 contractors. Firstly, considering that the rationality of procurement planning 
503 is the most crucial individual factor influencing the objective control of large 
504 international EPC projects, procurement planning must evolve beyond being a 
505 traditional, reactive "procure according to drawings" schedule. Instead, it 
506 should become a "strategic driver" that actively contributes to project success 
507 and enhances project value. EPC general contractors can establish a 
508 cross-functional team, led by the procurement manager, with core members 
509 including the design manager, construction manager, control manager, 
510 contract manager, and early-intervention key suppliers. This team is jointly 
511 responsible for the formulation, review, and updating of procurement plans. 
512 Secondly, given that "design leadership ability" holds the highest significance 
513 among the three core abilities, enhancing this ability serves as the strategic 
514 fulcrum for international EPC general contractors to attain core 
515 competitiveness. It is imperative for EPC general contractors to invest in a 
516 high-quality and innovative design team. The essence of design leadership 
517 ability lies in making design the creator of project value, the resolver of 
518 technical risks, and the driver of overall process efficiency. EPC general 
519 contractors can enhance their design originality ability by establishing an 
520 enterprise-level knowledge base and top technical expert pool, deepening 
521 front-end consulting, actively providing conceptual solutions for owners, and 
522 clearly demonstrating the value-added benefits that original design can bring 
523 to the entire project lifecycle. This approach enables minimizing project costs 
524 and maximizing efficiency through design optimization, all while ensuring 
525 functionality and safety, ultimately maximizing the overall project 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPTARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
526 objectives.Thirdly, the high emphasis on design integration management 
527 underscores the necessity of formal interface management. Leveraging 
528 information technologies like BIM and digital twins, it ensures the seamless 
529 and efficient flow of design information across the entire EPC chain, fosters 
530 integrated collaboration throughout the entire EPC project process, and 
531 prevents costly errors and delays in information transfer and handover, 
532 thereby enhancing the design leadership capability. Fourthly, given that 
533 supplier selection and management capability is the second pillar of 
534 international supply chain management, EPC general contractors can 
535 establish a certification supplier database through rigorous evaluation by 
536 constructing an evaluation system. For key equipment and materials, qualified 
537 suppliers from different continents or trade zones can be developed and 
538 certified to avoid the risk of trade wars, epidemics, or logistics disruptions in a 
539 single region. In addition, the supply model can be diversified by combining 
540 the use of single-source, multi-source, and alternative-source strategies. 
541 Single-source procurement is adopted for core technologies or brands with 
542 strong dependence, while multi-source procurement is adopted for general 
543 bulk materials to diversify risks. For supplier management, long-term 
544 framework agreements can be signed with strategic suppliers or strategic 
545 suppliers can be invited to intervene early to stimulate supplier loyalty and 
546 collaborative value, and jointly cope with risks. In addition, digital technology 
547 can be used to visualize the entire supplier performance process, provide 
548 early warnings, respond quickly, and prepare contingency plans for inevitable 
549 disruptions to minimize losses.Fifth, given that construction technical 
550 capability has been proven to be the most crucial factor in construction 
551 performance capability, in international EPC projects, the EPC general 
552 contractor's ability to build a high-level technical team that can both be deeply 
553 involved on-site and have a broad perspective, and the "hard power" to solve 
554 complex technical problems on-site, is the cornerstone to ensure the final 
555 successful delivery. At the same time, the use of modern information 
556 technology such as BIM can extend and amplify the capabilities of technical 
557 personnel, achieving intelligent construction with "human-machine 
558 collaboration", which is the fundamental guarantee for the project to 
559 ultimately achieve high-quality and high-efficiency project objectives.
560 The findings of this study strongly support the long-standing "front-end 
561 loading" principle in project management literature, emphasizing the 
562 dominant role of design leadership and international supply chain 
563 management capabilities and their impact on the ultimate project objectives. 
564 This confirms that in large international EPC projects, the cost of errors can 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPTARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
565 rise exponentially if not identified during the design and procurement process. 
566 However, this study also challenges the traditional and often implicit 
567 assumption that construction execution is the primary area of project control. 
568 The relatively low weight given to construction performance capabilities 
569 indicates a paradigm shift. While early research on the success factors of 
570 construction projects often emphasized on-site management and labor 
571 productivity, the findings of this study suggest that in the context of 
572 international EPCs, the role of contractors is shifting from construction 
573 executors to system integrators and coordinators. Core value creation and risk 
574 mitigation occur upstream, determined by design and supply chain strength. 
575 Another key difference from previous research is the strategic importance of 
576 supply chain management, particularly the rationality of procurement plans. 
577 While supply chain risks in international projects have been recognized, this 
578 study positions the rationality of procurement plans as the single most 
579 influential factor. This finding suggests that in today's fragmented and volatile 
580 global market, international supply chain management capabilities are 
581 increasingly important, extending the "front-end loading" theory from purely 
582 design technology to a technology-business combination.
583 Despite its contributions, this study has limitations that provide directions 
584 for future research. First, data collection mainly focuses on Chinese 
585 international EPC contractors and projects, which may introduce regional bias. 
586 The general applicability of weights to contractors in other regions (such as 
587 Europe and North America) needs to be verified. Future research can conduct 
588 cross-national comparative studies to validate and calibrate models under 
589 different cultural and industrial backgrounds. Second, the identification of 
590 objective control influencing factors relies on literature review and grounded 
591 theory coding of secondary data (such as case studies, reports). Although this 
592 method is rigorous, it may be affected by researchers' interpretative bias as 
593 well as the availability and quality of secondary data. Future research can 
594 adopt qualitative methods such as expert interviews and Delphi studies to 
595 further refine the list of factors and improve its validity. Third, this study 
596 provides a static snapshot of factor importance. The dynamic evolution of 
597 these weights at different project stages (such as feasibility, execution, and 
598 closure) has not been explored. Future research can conduct longitudinal 
599 studies to track how these capabilities change in their impact throughout the 
600 project lifecycle and provide management guidance at specific stages.
601Conclusions
602This study aims to systematically identify and quantitatively analyze the key 
ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPTARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
603factors influencing objective control in large international EPC projects. The 
604findings strongly suggest that design leadership and international supply chain 
605management constitute the two most crucial pillars of successful multi-objective 
606control, collectively accounting for over half of the total impact weight. This 
607empirically validates the core EPC principle of front-loading, shifting the 
608paradigm of project control from the traditional construction-centered focus to a 
609more strategic emphasis on upstream integrated processes.
610The primary theoretical contribution of this study lies in the development 
611and empirical validation of the EPC comprehensive management capability 
612model. This model transforms the abstract concept of "integration" in EPC 
613projects into a measurable, hierarchical framework consisting of 3 core 
614capabilities and 11 specific indicators, accompanied by their relative weights. It 
615significantly advances EPC governance theory by providing quantitative evidence 
616that effective governance in complex international environments depends not 
617only on overseeing construction but also on proactively coordinating 
618knowledge-intensive design capabilities and globally dispersed supply chain 
619management capabilities. The model challenges the implicit assumption that 
620construction execution is the main control area, positioning contractors as 
621system integrators whose competitive advantage stems from intellectual capital 
622in design and global network orchestration in supply chain management. 
623Practically, the study provides actionable insights for international contractors to 
624improve project performance in complex global environments. Firstly, 
625procurement planning must be elevated from a tactical schedule to a strategic 
626driver of project success, managed by cross-functional teams. Secondly, strategic 
627investment in high-quality, innovative design teams is crucial for creating value 
628and reducing risk. Thirdly, the importance of design integration highlights the 
629need for formal interface management supported by digital tools such as BIM to 
630ensure seamless information flow. Fourthly, contractors should develop robust 
631and diversified strategic supplier networks to enhance resilience. Finally, during 
632construction execution, prioritizing technical problem-solving capabilities over 
633general resource management is crucial for addressing remaining uncertainties.
634This study also has the following limitations. The data collection primarily 
635focuses on Chinese international EPC contractors, which may introduce regional 
636bias. Future research should validate the model in different cultural and 
637industrial contexts, such as Europe and North America. Additionally, this study 
638only provides the importance of factors in a static context; future longitudinal 
639research investigating how these weights evolve at different project stages can 
640provide more dynamic, stage-specific guidance.
