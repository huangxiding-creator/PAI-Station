## Abstract

This article examines the progressive development and codification of international construction contract law through the systematic incorporation of FIDIC standard conditions into the UNIDROIT and UNCITRAL model laws. It analyzes the interplay between private-law mechanisms (claims notification, performance bonds, retention funds) and public-law frameworks, focusing on harmonization of specialized terminology, fixed procedural time bars for claims, and the institutional role of engineer-decision-makers within multi-tiered Dispute Adjudication/Prevention Boards (DAB/DAAB). Particular attention is paid to the interface between “soft law” instruments issued by non-governmental and intergovernmental organizations and diverse national regulatory regimes, as well as the influence of contemporary arbitral jurisprudence on uniform contract interpretation and risk-allocation practices in cross-border infrastructure projects. Employing a comparative-legal and formal-legal methodology, the study integrates systemic analysis with doctrinal review of key investment and commercial arbitration awards applying FIDIC, UNIDROIT and UNCITRAL provisions to identify best practices in norm harmonization. The research demonstrates that embedding FIDIC clauses within the UNIDROIT and UNCITRAL model laws significantly mitigates legal fragmentation and enhances procedural predictability. Novel contributions include the codification of a uniform 28-day notice period for claims, formal recognition of engineer determinations as binding pre-arbitration measures, and a structured algorithm for transposing these provisions into national legislation. This approach clarifies contractual document hierarchy, balances stakeholder rights and obligations, and accelerates pre-arbitration dispute resolution. The findings confirm the practical viability of “soft law” integration for major international construction projects and reinforce its strategic role in the evolution of a cohesive global construction contract regime.
