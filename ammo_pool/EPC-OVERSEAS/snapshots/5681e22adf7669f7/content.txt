ARGENTINA │ On the Road to Recovery
8
The Argentine energy industry is undergoing a significant transformation, characterized by new commercial 
agreements, extensions of concessions, and plans for future diversification projects. These developments 
reflect ongoing efforts to address industry challenges, such as high energy costs and concerns regarding energy 
reliability, while taking advantage of opportunities to expand and diversify the country's energy mix. 
Furthermore, Argentina is pursuing various measures to improve its energy sector, including investing in new 
infrastructure to enhance the energy grid, increasing the deployment of renewable energy sources, and 
promoting energy efficiency. The government is also taking initiatives to attract foreign investment to the energy 
sector, with several new projects expected to come online soon.
2022 Developments
• Exploration and Production Extension: 
Several upstream concession extensions 
have been granted in Argentina, including 
CMA-1, CAN-100, and MLO-118.
• Boosting Export Capacity: Oldelval and 
Oiltanking have been granted a 10-year 
extension of their concession for pipelines 
and logistics hubs. Oldelval will invest 
$750MM to double its transport capacity 
from the Vaca Muerta shale formation to 
boost export capacity.
• Restarting Operations on the Oleoducto
Trasandino: The Oleoducto Trasandino, 
which can pump 115 kboepd, will resume 
operations to transport oil from Argentina to 
Chile.
• YPF and Petronas Gas Agreement: YPF 
and Petronas have signed a landmark 
agreement to produce more natural gas in 
Vaca Muerta and construct an LNG plant. 
YPF and Petronas said the project would 
take at least a decade to complete, after 
which it's expected to generate and export 
around 25 MM tonnes of LNG annually.​
• The Plan Gas Extended to 2028: Plan 
Gas incentivizes producers to bid for 
contracts and supply offtakes to increase 
natural gas production in Argentina.
• Vaca Muerta Pipeline Extension: The 
extension of the Vaca Muerta pipeline to 
Brazil is back on the table. The first stage of 
the gas pipeline, which will link 
Vaca Muerta with Buenos Aires, will be 
finished in June 2023.

BARBADOS │ Studies and Activities Progressing
10
Barbados is currently focused on transitioning towards sustainable energy sources as the country aims to achieve 
carbon neutrality by 2030. The government has set a target of generating 65% of electricity from renewables, though 
the country still faces high infrastructure costs and needs to modernize their energy grid. The impact of climate 
change on the island's energy security has enhanced the need to establish a robust regulatory frameworks to 
support industry growth. Barbados is diversifying its energy mix through investment in renewable projects and 
developing energy storage solutions.

BOLIVIA │ Rethinking Long-term Strategy
11
The Bolivian energy industry is dominated by natural gas, with the country historically having significant reserves and 
being a major commodity exporter. In recent years, the industry has faced challenges such as declining production 
and international demand for natural gas, as well as, political instability. The country is seeking to increase its 
domestic consumption of natural gas and develop its downstream industry, including the production of LNG. Political 
developments and economic factors will continue to impact the industry, with ongoing tensions between the 
government and private sector companies and fluctuating global energy prices. However, Bolivia has invested in 
renewable energy, intending to have 60% of its energy mix from renewables by 2030.
2022 Developments
• Repsol Largest Gas Producer 
well: Repsol successfully tested Margarita 
10, now the country's biggest gas producer, 
with a flow rate of 106 mmcfpd. The well 
was spudded in 2021, and its success is 
a positive development for Bolivia's natural 
gas industry, which has been facing 
declining production and demand in recent 
years.
• YPFB Exploration: YPFB plans to 
drill Mayaya Centro-X1 IE in November 
2023, which is expected to produce 
4 mmcfpd of gas. Additionally, YPFB will 
conduct seismic surveys in the 
Boomerang and Vitiacua areas, which are 
believed to have significant potential for 
oil and gas reserves.

BRAZIL │ Offshore Momentum and Onshore Revival
13
Brazil's energy industry, traditionally known for its vast oil reserves, is poised for continued growth in 2023, with 
increasing investment in offshore exploration, production, and renewable energy projects. However, the country must 
face regulatory uncertainty, an outdated grid infrastructure, and environmental concerns. Despite the pandemic, 
supply chain disruptions, and economic slowdowns, LAM's most significant market player remained resilient, with the 
country achieving a landmark production rate in 2022.
2022 Developments
• Landmark Production: Brazil achieved a 
landmark production rate of over 
3.9 MMboepd (3.1 Mmbo + 140 MMm3 
natural gas). Investments in offshore 
exploration and production, including in the 
pre-salt fields off Brazil's coast, drove this 
landmark achievement. These fields 
are known for their high-quality oil and gas 
reserves, which are located beneath thick 
layers of salt requiring specialized 
technology and expertise to extract.
• Deepwater Oil Field Production: Petrobras 
Roncador IOR, a deepwater oil field in the 
Campos Basin, successfully started 
production using the P62 FPSO, adding 
20 kboed to Brazil's oil.
• Open Acreage Bidding: Brazil's 3rd Round 
Open Acreage Bid was a successful auction. 
The open acreage production-sharing tender 
aims to contract exploration and production 
for oil and natural gas in pre-salt polygon 
and other strategic areas. Petrobras and 
IOCs acquired 4 offshore blocks, resulting in 
a total investment of 1.44 B reais 
and signing bonuses of 916 MM reais. 
However, the Permanent Sharing Offer was 
discontinued, consisting of the continuous 
supply of exploratory blocks and areas with 
marginal accumulations in land or sea 
basins.
• Successful Production in Campos 
Basin: Equinor Peregrino Phase II, 
offshore Campos Basin, achieved its first 
oil. At its peak, the field expects to produce 
up to 250 kboepd.
• New Petrobras CEO: Petrobras appointed 
new CEO, Jean Paul Prates, who is 
expected to bring a fresh perspective and 
help navigate the company through 
the changing landscape of the energy 
industry.

CHILE │ Focused on Renewables
15
The Chilean energy industry has experienced significant growth and transformation over the past decade, with a 
strong focus on renewable energy development. In 2022, the country set a new goal of achieving 100% renewable 
electricity by 2040, with additional plans to close all coal-fired power plants by 2040. Additionally, the 
government has launched an initiative to develop green hydrogen as a critical component of its energy mix, aiming to 
become a significant fuel exporter. The country is working to improve its energy grid infrastructure, including 
developing a 3,000 km transmission line to connect the northern and central grids. However, there have been 
challenges in achieving these goals, including social and environmental opposition to some projects and concerns 
around energy prices and grid stability.
2022 Developments
• Geothermal Exploration Licenses 
Awarded: Chile was the first country in the 
Southern Cone to introduce a carbon tax, in 
2014, aiming to reduce GHG emissions and 
promote clean energy. The tax imposes $5 
to $30 per ton of CO2 on fossil fuel power 
plants over 50 MW. Since the carbon 
tax implementation, Chile has been 
exploring its significant geothermal potential 
and has awarded exploration licenses to 3 
companies, with the first geothermal power 
plant expected to be operational by 2024.
• Green Hydrogen Strategy: In 2020, Chile 
unveiled a green hydrogen strategy to 
become a world leader in producing and 
exporting green hydrogen, investing 
$50MM in a production facility and aiming 
for $30B in investment by 2030. In 2021, an 
Energy Efficiency Law declared hydrogen 
gases as a "fuel."
• Energy Storage 
Law Enacted:  Chile's Energy Storage 
Law, enacted in 2020, mandates 
distribution companies to integrate energy 
storage systems into the country's 
electricity grid, enhancing reliability and 
reducing dependence on fossil fuel-based 
backup generators.
• Renewable Energy Project Funding: Chile
's renewable energy initiatives are gaining 
momentum, with CORFO selecting projects 
to receive $50MM in funding support. To 
generate 70% of electricity from renewable 
sources by 2030, the government launched 
a tender process in 2019 and announced 
funding support for renewable energy and 
energy efficiency projects in 2020 through 
CORFO.

COLOMBIA │ A Beacon in Latin America
17
In 2022, Colombia continued its efforts to transition to a more sustainable energy mix, with the government's target of 
achieving 14% of its energy generation from non-conventional renewable sources by 2025. However, the country 
has faced industry-reduced investment, low oil prices, regulatory changes and infrastructure limitations. The 
Colombian government has been working to address these challenges and create a more favorable investment 
climate for the offshore energy sector. However, the renewable energy environment is gaining momentum with 
investment into projects, innovation, and updated regulations. Additionally, Colombia had offshore discoveries and 
continues to build on exploration projects.

GEOTHERMAL │ Potential Heating Up Across LAM
Opportunities
• Non-Weather Dependent: Geothermal energy is not affected by weather 
conditions such as wind speeds or cloud cover fluctuations. Geothermal 
energy is more reliable and stable than other renewable energy sources.
• Various Applications: Geothermal energy can be used for multiple day-
to-day applications, including heating, and cooling buildings, generating 
electricity and providing hot water. These applications can help reduce 
greenhouse gas emissions and contribute to decarbonization efforts.
• Co-Production from Vintage Wells: Many geothermal resources are 
in areas with existing oil and gas wells. Co-production of geothermal 
energy alongside oil and gas extraction from vintage wells could help 
increase the efficiency of energy production and reduce the environmental 
impacts.
• CCS and Circular Economy Principles: Applying CCS technologies to 
geothermal production can provide cash flow via carbon credits and 
reduce the environmental impact. Additionally, using geothermal fluids to 
power industrial processes can reduce waste and increase efficiency.
Challenges
• High upfront cost of exploration drilling: The high upfront cost of 
exploration drilling can be a significant barrier to developing geothermal 
resources, particularly in the early stages when the resource is still being 
explored and tested. Solutions include maximizing renewable incentives 
and adopting oil and gas processes in geothermal exploration and 
production to reduce costs and increase efficiency.

• CCS: CCS technology has been gaining global acceptance as a 
decarbonization option. Globally, 30 CCS facilities are commercially 
operating, and another 11 are under construction. In addition, hundreds

of development.
• LAM Uniquely Positioned: LAM has thousands of drilled wells,

understanding provides several actionable opportunities 
including  repurposing depleted oil and gas fields for CCS storage.

in global trading as the world continues to focus on reducing GHG 
emissions. CCS investment will provide stronger positioning for future 
strategic opportunities.

starting studies and evaluations.

deployment along with clear determination of regulation and 
responsibilities are major factors to improve conditions and provide 
incentives for these long-term capital investments.
