# Environmental Mandated Delay: A Source-Based Classification Framework for Compliance-Driven Time Impacts in Construction Contracts

## Abstract

This article examines whether prevailing delay classifications in construction contracts adequately address time risk arising from mandatory environmental compliance obligations. It identifies a structural doctrinal gap in existing categories and proposes a distinct classification framework, environmental mandated delay (EMD), supported by a five-element cumulative test. A normative-comparative doctrinal methodology is employed, comprising (1) systematic analysis of delay provisions in major standard form construction contracts [the Fédération Internationale des Ingénieurs-Conseils (FIDIC) forms, the New Engineering Contract (NEC), and the Joint Contracts Tribunal (JCT) forms]; (2) comparative review of environmental regulatory frameworks in four jurisdictions (the UK, Australia, Indonesia, and the UAE); (3) a systematic review of published arbitral awards rendered under the auspices of the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID) and judicial decisions demonstrating recurring classification ambiguity; and (4) application of the proposed framework to documented infrastructure project contexts. None of the major standard form contracts articulates a delay category grounded in mandatory public law compliance. A structured content analysis of seven publicly available ICSID awards (2014–2022) confirms that compliance-based temporal disruption is recurrent across infrastructure disputes but no tribunal applied a structured delay classification framework in any case. Analysis of published arbitral decisions further identifies five recurring patterns that confirm the structural nature of the gap. EMD directly addresses each pattern by grounding entitlement in the public law source of the delaying obligation. The framework offers a structured analytical tool for extension-of-time (EOT) assessment, aligning delay classification with critical path–based scheduling analysis and improving consistency in contract administration and dispute resolution. The article makes three original contributions: (1) identification of a structural doctrinal gap supported by empirical arbitral evidence; (2) development of a source-based approach reorienting delay analysis from event- and actor-based models toward public law obligations; and (3) provision of an operational test applicable in engineering practice, enhancing predictability in the treatment of compliance-driven delay in climate-transition infrastructure projects.
