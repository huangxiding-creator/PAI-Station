1. Executive Summary of Statement of Case. 
1.1 The Contractor had signed an Agreement with Millennium Challenge Account 
(MCA), the Employer, for the modernisation and extension of Airport, a new Passenger 
Terminal Building and all associated Infrastructure since 2010.  
1.2 On 22 nd March 2012, the then ruling government and all its implementing agencies 
were overthrown in a military coup.  Millennium Challenge Compact (MCC) then elected to 
discontinue their financing of this Contract and summarily terminated their agreement with 
MCA. 
1.3 MCA suspended the Works on 30 th March, 2012 with no clear indicator as to the final 
solution to the contractual issues concomitant with its actions.  At that stage the Contractor 
preferred to believe that MCA and itself could resolve the problems surrounding the 
suspension amicably and even continue the remainder of the Works, which was 
approximately 65% complete at the time. 
1.4 MCA formally notified the Contractor of its termination of the Contract with 
immediate effect basing its reliance to do this on the COPA-Part B – Sub-Clause 15.2 (h). 
 
2. Contractor’s reasoning substantiating its entitlements to recompensable 
overhead costs plus profits, based on the provisions of the Contract and prevalent law.     
2.1 The Parties rights and obligations in the event of termination of a Contract are 
stipulated under Clauses 15 and 16. 
2.2 The Employer ’s rights to terminate “if the Contractor fails to carry out any 
obligation under the Contract” are specified under Clause 15 as follows: 
(i) Terminate the Contract for reason of the Contractor ’s failure to perform under any of 
the items of Sub-Clause 15.2 (a) to (f) of the GCC and COPA Sub-Clause (g) and (h).

(ii) Under Sub-clause 15.3, the Engineer shall evaluate, on behalf of the Employer, what 
is due to, or by, each Party. 
(iii) Under Sub-clause 15.5 the Employer may withhold such monies from the Contractor 
as are compensable to the Employer in terms of the Contract upon termination.  It should be 
noted that the provisions of the Contract under Clause 15 make only allowance for payment 
to the Contractor of such costs, but not profits, which are reasonably due to him.  Failure to 
perform any contractual obligations deemed to be a responsibility of the Contractor is 
construed to be a Contractor ’s Risk Event.  Accordingly it would hardly be appropriate to 
reward the Contractor with payments of profits, besides overhead costs, if he was initially in 
fundamental breach of the Contract by failing to perform. Conversely the opposite is also true. 
The Contractor cannot by implication be denied his rights to profits for an Event deemed to 
be ERE.   
(iv) The Employer is entitled to terminate the Contract for his own convenience in terms 
of Sub-Clause 15.5, which by all appearances happens to have been the case, albeit Sub-
Clause 15.2 (h) has been added to the GCC to cater for an event that “the Compact expires, is 
suspended or terminates (sic) in whole or in part in accordance with the terms of the 
Compact”. 
Though more particular emphasis is placed by MCA on Sub-Clause 15.2(h) as its basis for 
right to terminate, it would be more applicable and appropriate if the reason for invocation of 
this was objectively evaluated.  The simple truth of the matter is that Sub-clause 15.2(h) was 
invoked by the Employer because of termination of funding by MCC as a result of a force 
majeure situation which had occurred as a result of the military coup on 22nd March, 2012.  
(v) The Employer MCA, though not expressly mentioning it, probably for excusable 
reasons of its own, has been forced to terminate the Contract because of the force majeure 
situation which had arisen on 22.03.2012.  This is best described under Sub-Clause 19.1 item 
(i). Whether the Employer did or did not perform its duty to notify the Contractor of its 
inability to perform all its payment obligations, retrospective to the military coup, does not 
deter from the reality.  A military coup, constituting a force majeure event, had de facto taken 
place regardless of whatever other terms were used to couch the wording of this event.

(vi)   It is common cause between the Parties that a force majeure event had occurred and still 
continues to prevail today.  Sub-Clause 17.3 encapsulates all the events under the Contract 
that constitute an Employer’s Risk Event (ERE). 
 Prominent amongst these are those events described under Sub-Clause 17.3, items (a) 
and (b) which describes aptly the occurrence of the military coup on 22 nd March, 2012.  It is 
therefore deemed that there is no dispute about this event being a ERE. 
 It would also be more appropriate to evaluate the Contractor ’s entitlements to 
overhead costs and profits taking into consideration that he bears no culpability, or Risk for 
the Event giving rise to his entitlements of the aforementioned. 
(vii) There is an implied principle under the terms of the Contract that when the Contractor 
is not in default of carrying out his obligations under the Contract, that “the Employer shall 
promptly pay to the Contractor the amount of any loss of profit or other loss or damage 
sustained by the Contractor as a result of this termination ” under Sub-Clause 16.4, which 
not been amended in the Particular Conditions or any other document.  Accordingly Sub-
Clause 16.4 (a), (b) and (c) are very much still related to this Agreement. 
( viii) Sub-Clause 15.2(h) of the Particular Conditions states in the last paragraph quote “ In 
the event of Employer termination of the Contract pursuant to Sub-paragraph (h) the 
Contractor shall be paid  in accordance with Sub-Clause 19.6, (Optional Termination, 
Payment and Release) in accordance with the terms of the Compact and any related 
agreements”.  (Underlining is by the Contractor for emphasis)   Every Clause and Sub-
Clause constitutes part of what can be termed related agreements of the GCC or Particular 
Conditions.  Read severally through the provisions of the Contract, payment of overhead 
costs and profits is an entitlement to the Contractor in terms of Sub-Clause 16.4 (a) to (d) 
when the Contractor has not breached his performance obligations as Sub-Clause 16.4 is an 
agreement related to Sub-Clause 15.2 (h) and 19.6.
