## [Current status of prostate cancer screening research].

*2026 | 10.19723/j.issn.1671-167x.2026.04.002*

原文: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42493433

**摘要/描述摘录:**
> Prostate cancer is the second most common malignant tumor among men worldwide. Its early diagnosis relies on screening. How to increase the detection rate of clinically significant prostate cancer, avoid overdiagnosis and over-treatment of low-risk tumors, while also considering economic benefits, has become a core issue in the field of screening. Currently, international prostate cancer screening strategies are moving from a single prostate-specific antigen (PSA) screening to individualized risk stratification, and exploring the roles of various stratification tools, such as magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), new biomarkers, and risk prediction models. However, there is no universally recognized unified approach. In recent years, although the incidence of prostate cancer in China has increased rapidly, the early diagnosis rate is still low. This is directly related to the insufficient prevalence of prostate cancer screening in China. Moreover, China also faces problems such as the lack of a reasonable screening model for elderly high-risk populations, insufficient public awareness, and the absence of a refined screening path. Future work involves building stratified screening strategies suitable for the Chinese population based on Chinese data, promoting the transition of screening from opportunistic to systematic, and is a task that needs to be carried out.

## [Chronic pain: from central sensitization to network homeostasis imbalance].

*2026 | 10.12122/j.issn.1673-4254.2026.10.24*

原文: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42934116

**摘要/描述摘录:**
> Following a "mechanism-technology-translation" framework and focusing on high-quality studies published between 2023 and 2025, this review critically synthesizes representative findings on network-level remodeling in central sensitization, the neuroimmune axis, energy metabolism and mitochondrial homeostasis, epigenetic reprogramming, and clinical translation in chronic pain. The onset and maintenance of chronic pain involve multi-level coupling among neurons, glial cells, the immune system, and epigenetic regulatory mechanisms. The core pathological feature can be characterized as the deviation of pain-regulatory networks from their normal functional range across molecular, cellular, and circuit levels, resulting in a self-sustaining abnormal steady state-termed "network homeostasis imbalance." Major bottlenecks include the absence of integrative network-level pathological models, systematic discrepancies between animal models and clinical phenotypes, insufficient cross-cohort reproducibility of biomarkers, and inadequate translational pathways from basic discoveries to clinical validation. Chronic pain research and management need to shift from a "single-target, single-pathway" paradigm toward network-level systems analysis. Establishing actionable linkages among mechanistic analysis, technological validation, and clinical implementation is key to advancing chronic pain management from empirical analgesia toward precision intervention.
